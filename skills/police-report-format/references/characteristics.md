# Characteristics (PC1–PC10)

Qualities of a good Police Incident Report. Violations are ADVISORY findings
— they inform the user and ride into the final document's advisory appendix;
they never block a group.

| ID | Characteristic | A good report… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PC1 | Accurate | …states facts that are correct: names spelled, numbers, dates, and times verified against the source. |
| PC2 | Objective | …records facts and observations, not opinions or conclusions; an officer's inference is labeled as an inference, never stated as established fact. |
| PC3 | Complete | …answers who, what, when, where, and how, and substantively populates every Required attribute. |
| PC4 | Clear | …uses plain language a non-specialist reader can follow; radio codes, abbreviations, and jargon are expanded on first use. |
| PC5 | Chronological | …tells the narrative in the order events occurred, past tense throughout. |
| PC6 | Attributed | …sources every fact — observed by the officer, or reported by a named party; hearsay is marked as such (see definitions.md). |
| PC7 | Concise | …carries information in every sentence; no filler, no editorializing. |
| PC8 | Consistent | …keeps parties, times, and locations in agreement across every section. |
| PC9 | Impartial | …describes conduct without characterizing the people involved beyond what was observed or reported. |
| PC10 | No placeholders | …contains no empty, TBD, or placeholder values in Required attributes. |
